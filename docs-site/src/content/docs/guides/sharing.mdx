Sharing is the other side of [routing](/guides/routing/). Routing decides
which computer this one asks. Sharing decides whether a computer answers
requests from the others, who it is offered to, and how much work it takes at
once. A computer you set up as the one that runs the model is shared with
your other computers by default.

## The switch on the computer

Each computer keeps one switch of its own: whether it lends itself out at all.
It is on unless you turn it off.

<Tabs syncKey="mode">
	<TabItem label="In the Waired app">
		Select the **Waired icon**, then **Inference**. The item reads **Share
		this computer** or **Stop sharing this computer**, and the **Sharing:**
		line below it reports the outcome: **enabled**, **disabled**, **paused**
		while the app is closed, or **nobody, set in the console** when the
		switch is on but the console has taken this computer out of every
		distribution.
	</TabItem>
	<TabItem label="In the terminal">
		```sh
		waired share on
		waired share off        # stop serving everyone, cutting off work running now
		waired share status
		```

		`status` reports the whole picture:

		```text
		Sharing this computer: on
		Your other computers: on
		Your team: off
		People outside your account: off
		Who this computer is shared with is set in the Waired console.
		```
	</TabItem>
</Tabs>

Turning the switch off stops every kind of serving at once. Your other
computers stop being answered, anyone using this computer from outside your
account is cut off, and requests running at that moment are not finished.
Your own use of this computer is unaffected. Nothing in the web console can
turn this switch back on.

## Who the computer is offered to

While the switch is on, the [web console](/guides/web-console/) decides who
the computer is offered to. Open the computer's page and find the **Sharing**
card. It has two switches, and a third while you are in a team:

- **Your other computers.** The rest of the computers on your account may
  send work here. Turning it off keeps this computer for the person sitting
  at it, and also turns Team share and Public share off.
- **Public share.** Other Waired users, public users, may run work on this
  computer while it has spare room. Off unless you turn it on. Read
  [Public Share](/public-share/) first.

When you are in a team, the card has a third switch, **Team share**, which
lends the computer to your teammates. Off unless you turn it on. Read
[Team Share](/team-share/) first.

The switches are linked. Turning **Your other computers** off also turns
Public share off, because a computer you are not lending to your own machines
is not lent to strangers either. Turning Public share on turns **Your other
computers** on. Team share is linked the same way.

## How much work it takes at once

The computer's page in the web console also sets its capacity.

| Setting | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Requests at once** | How many requests this computer answers at the same time. More lets it work in parallel, but each one reserves its own VRAM for the KV cache and slows every request. `0` means automatic. The console shows the figure that applies beside the field, and the recommended maximum that fits in VRAM, and asks you to confirm before you go above it. |
| **Public share limit** | How many public users may use the computer at once. `0` means automatic: one is kept for you, and public users take up to 2. You can raise it up to what the computer takes at once. |
| **Main conversation** and **Sub-agents** (under **Which requests** on the Sharing card) | Which kinds of request from Claude Code and OpenCode this computer takes from everyone it is shared with. Its own Claude Code and OpenCode are never affected. Turning **Main conversation** off keeps a slower computer for sub-agent work only. |
| **Routing priority** | Which of your computers is tried first when several could answer. **High** is chosen first, **Low** serves only when no higher-priority computer can, and **Middle** is the default. |

<Aside type="note" title="Your own work comes first">
	Whatever the numbers say, the person at the computer takes priority when
	it is busy. A public user never blocks you for long, and new public work
	is paused while you are using the computer.
</Aside>

## What quitting the app does

**Quit** on the Waired icon stops the engine and stops all sharing for as
long as the app is closed. Nobody is sent work to this computer while nobody
is at the keyboard. Starting the app again puts everything back as it was, and
the sharing setting itself is never changed by quitting. Logging out of your
computer or shutting it down does the same.
