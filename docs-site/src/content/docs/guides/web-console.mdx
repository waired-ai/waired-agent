The Waired app applies to the computer you are sitting at. The web console
applies to all of your computers, from any browser, including a phone. Open
[app.waired.ai](https://app.waired.ai) and sign in with the same account.
From the Waired app, **Open Waired console…** opens it signed in.

The console's chrome is in English. Its pages are available in English and
Japanese, and it follows your browser's language.

## Devices

The list you land on. One row per computer, with what it is running and
whether it is reachable right now.

| Column | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| **Status** | Whether that computer is online and reachable. |
| **Inference** | Whether it runs models itself, and its state: **Ready**, **Inference off**, or a problem. |
| **Peers** | How many of your other computers it can talk to right now. |
| **Last seen** | When it last reported in. |
| **Sign-in expires** | When that computer's sign-in expires, if expiry is on. |

A computer whose setup never finished shows a banner that links to its setup
page. See [Set up in the browser](/getting-started/set-up-in-the-browser/).

## One computer's page

Select a row.

### Overview

The computer's name, hardware, operating system, Waired version, address, and
when it was last seen. **Rename** it here. The name is only for your benefit,
and changing it breaks nothing.

### Model

Which model this computer runs, and **Change model** to switch it. The new
model downloads in the background, the progress appears here, and the current
model keeps answering until the new one is ready. On a computer that runs
vLLM, the engine then restarts to load it, and the computer doesn't answer
until it's ready. A computer that runs no
model of its own does not have this section.

### Sharing

Who this computer is offered to, as three switches: **Your other computers**,
**Team share** (while you are in a team) and **Public share**. Beside each
switch a badge says what is happening: **Shared**, **Not shared**, or **Off on
the computer** while the computer's own switch is off. The switches are
linked: turning **Your other computers** off also turns Team share and Public
share off, and turning either of those on turns **Your other computers** on.
See [Team Share](/team-share/) and [Public Share](/public-share/).

The card shows, but cannot change, the switch the computer keeps for itself.
While that switch is off, a banner at the top of the card reads **Sharing is
off on this computer**, and turning it back on happens on the computer, with
**Share this computer** in the Waired app or `waired share on`. See
[Share a computer with your other devices](/guides/sharing/).

Under **Which requests**, two more switches, **Main conversation** and
**Sub-agents**, decide which Claude Code and OpenCode requests this computer
takes from everyone it is shared with. Its own Claude Code and OpenCode always
work.

### Capacity

**Requests at once** is how many requests this computer answers at the same
time, and **Public share limit** how many of those public users may take. The
figure that applies is shown beside each field. **Routing priority** decides
which of your computers is tried first, and **Keep model in memory** how long
the model stays loaded after the last request. For what each one does, see
[Share a computer with your other devices](/guides/sharing/#how-much-work-it-takes-at-once).

<Aside type="note" title="Concurrency costs memory">
	Raising **Requests at once** lets more work run at once, but each
	simultaneous request reserves its own VRAM and makes every request slower.
	The console shows a recommended maximum for this computer and asks you to
	confirm before going above it.
</Aside>

### Authentication

When this computer's sign-in expires, and whether expiry applies to it. You
can also disable a computer temporarily, so it stops being usable without
being removed, or delete it.

<Aside type="caution" title="Delete is for computers you no longer have">
	Deleting removes the computer from your network from the outside. If you
	still have the computer, [uninstall Waired](/getting-started/uninstall/)
	on it instead. That removes it cleanly from both sides. A deleted computer
	that is still running Waired stops working until it signs in again.
</Aside>

## Public share

A summary across your whole account: which of your computers are shared with
public users, and what you have been using. Each computer links to its sharing
settings, and **Stop sharing** here cuts off work public users are running on
it right now. See [Public Share](/public-share/).

## Settings

Your network's name, whether sign-ins expire and after how long, which sign-in
providers your account accepts, and your auth keys. See
[Set up a server with an auth key](/getting-started/servers-and-auth-keys/).

## Logs

A record of things that changed and when: a computer joining, being renamed,
disabled or deleted, routing or capacity being changed, keys rotated, and the
choices made during setup. This is where to look when something changed and
you are not sure what changed it.

## Account

Your display name and account ID, and the current session. It also holds
**Delete account**, which removes the account and everything in it and cannot
be undone.

<Expected tone="bad" title="A computer is missing from the list">
	It never finished signing in, or it was deleted. On the computer itself,
	run `waired status`. If it reports no identity, sign it in again. See
	[Sign in](/getting-started/sign-in/).
</Expected>
